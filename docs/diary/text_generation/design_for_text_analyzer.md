## Core terminology

### Proposed terms

#### OUTCOME

A representation of what the text communicates, asserts,
presupposes, commits to, or otherwise establishes as a
linguistically relevant consequence of the utterance.

#### REALIZATION

The linguistic and discourse-level choices and mechanisms
through which an OUTCOME is expressed, conveyed, or interpreted.

The terms OUTCOME and REALIZATION are provisional operational
terms introduced for this proposal. They are not intended to
correspond directly to established linguistic categories.

## Relation to terms in linguistics

### Semantics

#### Definition

Semantics is the study of the meaning of linguistic
expressions, including how meanings are associated with
words and other expressions and how they combine to
determine the meaning of larger expressions.

In formal approaches, semantics includes the systematic
characterization of interpretations and, in particular,
truth conditions.

#### Relation to the proposal

Semantics is primarily related to OUTCOME in this framework.

The semantic content of an utterance contributes to the
representation of what the text communicates and what it
asserts or establishes.

However, OUTCOME is intentionally broader than Semantics.
It may also include linguistically relevant consequences
that are described in pragmatic terms, such as presuppositions,
implicatures, communicative acts, or interpersonal consequences.

Therefore, `Semantics ≠ OUTCOME` and `OUTCOME` includes `Semantics` as a subset. 

Semantics is treated as one of the principal linguistic perspectives used to characterize OUTCOME.

### Pragmatics

#### Definition

Pragmatics is commonly described as the study of language
use in context. It concerns how contextual factors interact
with linguistic meaning in the interpretation of utterances.

Pragmatics therefore concerns not only what a linguistic
expression means, but what a speaker communicates by using
that expression in a particular context.

Central topics include implicature, deixis, speech acts,
presupposition, and context-dependent interpretation.
The precise boundaries of pragmatics vary across theoretical
traditions.

#### Relation to the proposal

Pragmatics is primarily related to OUTCOME in this framework.

Pragmatic analysis provides concepts for describing what an
utterance communicates, presupposes, implies, commits to,
or accomplishes in a particular communicative context.

However, pragmatic concepts may also describe the mechanisms
or strategies through which an OUTCOME is realized or
interpreted.

Therefore, `Pragmatics ≠ OUTCOME` and `Pragmatics` is not a subset of `OUTCOME`. 

Rather, Pragmatics is treated as an important source of
concepts for characterizing OUTCOME, while some pragmatic
phenomena may also be relevant to REALIZATION.

In particular, concepts such as politeness, deixis, and
information structure may involve both an outcome and the
means by which that outcome is achieved or interpreted.
Such concepts should not necessarily be assigned exclusively
to either OUTCOME or REALIZATION.
